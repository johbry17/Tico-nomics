Data Audit and Source Reconciliation

Phase 1 — Establishing a Trustworthy Analytical Foundation

Published

November 2, 2026

1. Research purpose and scope

This audit establishes a reliable analytical foundation for investigating how the World Bank’s financing relationship with Costa Rica has evolved since 1980.

The research question is not simply the history of World Bank lending to Costa Rica. Costa Rica is the case study; the subject is the evolution of international development finance and priorities. The portfolio illuminates shifts in development thinking — from structural adjustment and infrastructure investment toward policy-based lending, fiscal reform, and climate integration — using Costa Rica as a well-documented, analytically tractable case.

The analysis must distinguish:

  1. Changes in the Bank’s own institutional strategies and priorities
  2. Changes specific to Costa Rica’s development trajectory and financing needs
  3. Changes attributable to Costa Rica’s creditworthiness and eligibility for different instruments

Costa Rica is upper-middle-income, institutionally capable, and environmentally distinctive. It is not a representative developing country, and findings should not be generalised as if it were.

The proposed primary study period is 1980–2025, with 2026 records retained separately as an incomplete period through the snapshot date. Pre-1980 records are preserved for audit purposes but excluded from primary analysis.

The 72-vs-115 discrepancy explained

The WB Projects API V2 (countrycode=CR, rows=100) returned 72 records. The Projects & Operations Excel contains 115 records. All 72 API records are confirmed subsets of the 115 Excel records. The 43 additional Excel records are explained by four categories:

Category A — Legacy pre-API records (19 projects): P006xxx-numbered projects approved between 1985–1999, mostly fully repaid, that are not indexed in the V2 API search results. These include SAL III (1993), water supply (1993), basic education (1991), and several cancelled adjustment loans.

Category B — Grants and non-IBRD operations (6 projects): GEF trust fund grants, carbon finance operations, and bilateral trust funds. These have Financing Type = Grants or Other in the Excel. They do not appear in the IBRD loan dataset and were not returned by the IBRD-focused API query.

Category C — Pre-1990 IBRD loans, fully repaid (18 projects): The V2 API only returned records for projects with P-numbers assigned during the modern project-management system era (post-1985 approximately). Earlier projects with P006xxx numbers were included in the Excel but not the API.

Category D — Post-1990 IBRD loans missing from the API (the critical case): Eight projects with Financing Type = IBRD that have loan records in the Statement, but were absent from the 72-record API extract. Seven are early-1990s projects that were apparently missed from the API’s search index. The eighth — P148435 ($420 million, 2016) — is the most significant discovery of this audit.

P148435 Strengthening Universal Health Insurance in Costa Rica 2016 1 $420M $0M $420M Repaying

P148435 is a $420 million Investment Project Financing (Specific Investment Loan) for the Costa Rican Social Security Institute (CCSS), approved by the Board on March 15, 2016. It is:

  • Present in the Projects & Operations Excel
  • Present in the IBRD Statement of Loans (loan IBRD85930, Repaying status, fully disbursed)
  • Absent from the 72-record V2 API extract

Its absence from the earlier analysis caused the incorrect conclusion that the 2015–2019 period had no substantial World Bank lending to Costa Rica. The $420M health insurance loan is the largest single IBRD commitment to Costa Rica in the entire 1980–2025 period.

Implication for earlier gap analysis: The Phase 0/1 analysis (from date-recovery.qmd) concluded the “2015–2019 gap” was “a real pattern.” That conclusion requires revision: 2016 had $420M in IBRD commitments, making it one of the highest-volume years in the portfolio. The genuine gap years in 2013–2019 are 2013, 2015, 2017, and 2019 — not 2013–2019 as a whole.

4. Financial definitions

IBRD Statement field definitions

The IBRD Statement of Loans and Guarantees is a current-status snapshot as of September 30, 2026. The financial fields represent cumulative balances, not annual flows.

Field Definition What it is NOT
original_principal_amount Loan amount as approved by the Board in nominal USD at approval Not money received; not disbursement
cancelled_amount Portions cancelled — unused balances at close, mid-project cancellations, or full cancellation of Terminated loans Not the same as undisbursed
undisbursed_amount Remaining approved amount not yet drawn down —
disbursed_amount Cumulative drawdowns since loan inception Not an annual flow; do not assign to approval year
repaid_to_ibrd Cumulative principal repayments —
due_to_ibrd Outstanding principal owed —
loans_held IBRD’s net exposure (disbursed − repaid − sold) —

Critical methodological constraint: disbursed_amount is a cumulative balance as of 09/30/2026. It cannot be assigned to the approval year or presented as an annual disbursement flow. For annual trend analysis, original_principal_amount (commitment at board approval year) is the appropriate primary measure.

The net principal measure

The derived measure net_principal = original_principal_amount − cancelled_amount is analytically useful but must be described precisely: it represents the original commitment net of all cancellations recorded through 09/30/2026. It is not “money received” or “money disbursed.” For Terminated loans where the loan agreement was never signed, both original principal and cancelled amount are identical, and net principal equals zero.

IDA

Costa Rica crossed the IDA income threshold and graduated from IDA eligibility approximately in 1993–1994. The IBRD Statement does not show any IDA transactions for Costa Rica. IDA lending to Costa Rica in the 1980s is theoretically possible but appears absent from the dataset. The WB IDA Statement of Credits and Grants is available at thedocs.worldbank.org and could be checked if pre-1990 coverage requires verification.

IFC

IFC operates actively in Costa Rica (private financial sector, sustainable agriculture, tourism). IFC project-level data is available from the IFC project portal. However, IFC investment amounts (loans, equity, quasi-equity to private entities) are not comparable to IBRD sovereign loan principal and must not be summed into a single financing total. If IFC is included in Phase 2, it should be reported as a separate indicator.

MIGA

MIGA issues political-risk guarantees to private investors, not sovereign loans. Its financial measure is “gross exposure” (face value of guarantees), not disbursed or committed loan amounts. MIGA data is available at miga.org. Given Costa Rica’s relatively stable investment environment, MIGA exposure is expected to be limited. Recommended as a scope note only.

What the current dataset covers and excludes

The analysis covers: IBRD sovereign loans and guarantee instruments to the Republic of Costa Rica, as reported in the IBRD Statement of Loans and Guarantees.

The analysis excludes: IDA (none for CR in the dataset), IFC private-sector investments, MIGA political-risk guarantees, bilateral aid flows, IMF programs, Inter-American Development Bank lending, and Costa Rica’s domestic borrowing.

Three loans approved in 2025 are in the dataset: P504033 (May 2025, $120M agriculture), P179757 (August 2025, $0 FCPF grant), and P508347 (September 2025, $300M fiscal DPL). These are within the 1980–2025 primary window and should be included as the most recent data point.

Two projects approved after September 30, 2026 may not yet appear in the dataset. The snapshot should be treated as approximately current through late 2026.

8. Inflation adjustment methodology

The nominal-dollar comparison of loans across a 46-year period (1980–2025) significantly misrepresents real scale. A $100M loan in 1980 represents approximately $328M in 2025 dollars using the US GDP deflator.

Why GDP deflator rather than CPI

  • CPI measures changes in consumer prices affecting US households. It is the standard measure for assessing purchasing power of wages and household budgets.
  • GDP deflator measures price changes across all US-produced final goods and services — a broader index that better reflects the purchasing power of US-dollar-denominated international transactions.

For this project, the GDP deflator is preferred because the aim is to compare the real scale of historical lending commitments, not specifically household purchasing power.

Proposed calculation

real_amount_2025 = nominal_amount × (defl_2025 / defl_approval_year)

where defl_x is the NY.GDP.DEFL.ZS value for year x.

Limitations

  1. Neither the US GDP deflator nor CPI measures the local purchasing power of lending in Costa Rica. A $100M loan in 1985 purchased different quantities of Costa Rican infrastructure than $100M in 2020.
  2. The 2025 deflator value (122.4) is preliminary and may be revised in future World Bank data vintages.
  3. Exchange-rate changes between the USD and Costa Rican colón are not captured.
  4. For Phase 2: preserve original nominal amounts as the primary field; real amounts should be a clearly labelled derived field.

Unresolved: earlier gap-analysis findings that need revision

The Phase 0 EDA report concluded:

“Years 2013, 2015, 2016, 2017, and 2019 still have zero confirmed positive IBRD financing commitments.”

This finding must be revised in light of the audit:

  • 2016 had $420M in IBRD commitments (P148435, health insurance loan) — one of the largest in the entire portfolio.
  • 1990 had $60M (P006926, Transport Sector Investment) and 1991–1993 had further loans, all absent from the earlier API-only analysis.

The corrected finding is: the genuinely low-activity years within 2013–2019 are 2013, 2015, 2017, and 2019 — not the entire period.

10. Recommended analytical dataset architecture

For Phase 2 analysis, the following data architecture is recommended:

Dataset Source Unit Primary use
loan_records_clean.csv IBRD Statement (filtered for CR, cleaned) Loan-level Financial detail; multi-loan project inspection
project_finance_summary.csv Aggregated from loan records Project-level Commitment totals; per-project analysis
projects_master.csv Reconciled from Excel + API + loans Project-level Canonical project catalogue; sector, instrument, date
project_source_reconciliation.csv Three-way reconciliation Project-level Audit trail; membership flags
us_gdp_deflator.csv World Bank API (NY.GDP.DEFL.ZS) Annual Inflation adjustment

Primary financial measure for trend analysis: original_principal_amount from the IBRD Statement, aggregated to project level, assigned to board_approval_year. This maps cleanly to calendar years and is not affected by the snapshot-date limitation of disbursement figures.

Scope filter for primary analysis: approval_year >= 1980 and approval_year <= 2025, is_guarantee = FALSE, !is.na(project_id). This gives 35 projects with IBRD loan records in the primary study window.
